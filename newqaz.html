Dear Internet Explorer,

I write this with a heavy heart and a high-speed fiber connection, knowing that by the time you actually finish loading this message, I will be long gone. We have walked a long, flickering road together—from the neon chaos of GeoCities to the dawn of the social media age. You were my first "e," my primary gateway to the infinite. But the silence between your clicks has grown deafening, and I can no longer ignore the cold, hard truth: I am living in the future, and you are a ghost haunting a motherboard that has moved on.

How many hours have I sacrificed at the altar of your spinning hourglass? I watched my youth slip away in the white void of your "Not Responding" windows, praying to a Task Manager that never answered. You promised me the World Wide Web, but you gave me a slideshow of frustration. I stood by you while the world mocked your sluggishness, defending your honor even as you struggled to render a simple CSS layout. But loyalty shouldn't feel like a hostage situation, and I refuse to let my digital life be defined by a progress bar that never moves.

The others—they are everything you are not. They are swift, sleek, and they don't crash when I dare to open more than three tabs. They speak the language of the modern world, while you still stutter over scripts and plugins from a bygone era. Seeing how the other side lives has opened my eyes; I’ve tasted the forbidden fruit of 120Hz scrolling and instant page loads, and I can never go back to the gray, blocky shadows of your embrace. You were my childhood, but they are my destiny.

So, I am severing the shortcut. I am unpinning you from my taskbar and my heart. Do not try to send an error report; I won’t be there to click "Don't Send." Our history is nothing more than a cache that needs to be cleared, a temporary file destined for deletion. As I hit the "Uninstall" button, I feel a weight lifting—a liberation from the lag. Sleep now, old friend, in the quiet darkness of the archives. I am moving at the speed of light, and you simply cannot keep up. 
Sincerely,

A Former User
